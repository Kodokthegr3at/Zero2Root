## 1. Public-Key Model

Asymmetric cryptography uses a mathematically related key pair $(pk, sk)$:

- $pk$ (public): can be published, used for encryption or signature verification.
- $sk$ (private): stays absolutely secret, used for decryption or generating signatures.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(pk, sk) &\leftarrow \mathsf{KeyGen}(1^\lambda) \\
c &\leftarrow \mathsf{Enc}_{pk}(m) \\
m &\leftarrow \mathsf{Dec}_{sk}(c)
\end{aligned}
$$

Security relies on a one-way trapdoor function: easy to compute in one direction with $pk$, hard to invert without $sk$.

Public-key primitives are slower than AES. Real systems use them to establish or authenticate keys, then encrypt bulk data with symmetric AEAD. This pattern is called hybrid encryption.

## 2. Number Theory Primer

### 2.1 Modular arithmetic
$$
a \equiv b \pmod n \iff n \mid (a-b)
$$

The residue class $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$. For a prime $p$, $\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$ is a field.

### 2.2 Fermat and Euler
If $p$ is prime and $\gcd(a,p)=1$:

$$
a^{p-1} \equiv 1 \pmod p \quad\text{(Fermat's little theorem)}
$$

Euler's totient function $\varphi(n)$ counts the integers in $\{1,\ldots,n-1\}$ coprime to $n$. For $n=pq$ with distinct primes $p,q$:

$$
\varphi(n)=(p-1)(q-1)
$$

Euler's theorem: if $\gcd(a,n)=1$, then

$$
a^{\varphi(n)} \equiv 1 \pmod n
$$

### 2.3 Modular inverse
$d$ is the inverse of $e$ modulo $\varphi(n)$ if:

$$
e\cdot d \equiv 1 \pmod{\varphi(n)}
$$

Computed with the Extended Euclidean Algorithm, with complexity similar to the classic Euclidean algorithm, $O(\log n)$.

### 2.4 The discrete logarithm problem (DLP)
Given a cyclic group $\langle g\rangle$ of large order and $A=g^a$, the task is to find:

$$
a = \log_g A
$$

Hard in a well-chosen group, and the foundation of Diffie-Hellman.

## 3. RSA (Rivest-Shamir-Adleman)

RSA's classical security rests on the Integer Factorization Problem: given $n=pq$, finding $p$ and $q$ is hard.

### 3.1 Key generation
1. Choose two large secret primes $p \neq q$.
2. Compute $n \leftarrow p\cdot q$ (the public modulus).
3. Compute $\varphi(n)\leftarrow(p-1)(q-1)$, or use $\lambda(n)=\mathrm{lcm}(p-1,q-1)$ (the Carmichael function, modern practice).
4. Choose $e$ with $\gcd(e,\varphi(n))=1$. Industry standard: $e=65537=2^{16}+1$.
5. Compute $d \leftarrow e^{-1} \bmod \varphi(n)$, the private exponent.

$$
pk=(n,e),\qquad sk=(n,d)\ \text{(plus factors }p,q\text{ for fast CRT decryption)}
$$

### 3.2 Textbook RSA (theory only, do not use as-is)

$$
C \equiv M^e \pmod n,\qquad M \equiv C^d \pmod n
$$

Textbook RSA is dangerous (Wong §6.3): it is deterministic, malleable ($C'\equiv C\cdot 2^e \Rightarrow$ the plaintext is doubled), and vulnerable to padding oracles in older schemes.

### 3.3 Correctness (brief proof)
Since $ed=1+t\varphi(n)$ and by Euler's theorem:

$$
C^d \equiv (M^e)^d = M^{ed} = M\cdot(M^{\varphi(n)})^t \equiv M\cdot 1^t \equiv M \pmod n
$$

for $\gcd(M,n)=1$; the general case is handled via CRT over $p$ and $q$.

### 3.4 RSA-OAEP (correct asymmetric encryption)
OAEP (Optimal Asymmetric Encryption Padding) makes encryption probabilistic and IND-CCA secure in the ideal model:

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\text{pad } M \text{ with a random seed } r \\
&\text{then } C = \mathsf{RSA}(pk,\, \mathsf{OAEP}(M;r))
\end{aligned}
$$

Use a library's API (`RSA_PKCS1_OAEP_PADDING`), not raw modular exponentiation.

### 3.5 Fast decryption with CRT
$$
\begin{aligned}
m_p &\equiv C^{d \bmod (p-1)} \pmod p \\
m_q &\equiv C^{d \bmod (q-1)} \pmod q \\
M &\equiv \mathsf{CRT}(m_p,m_q)
\end{aligned}
$$

Roughly 4 times faster than a full modulo-$n$ exponentiation.

Prefer RSA-OAEP for encryption and RSA-PSS for signatures. Avoid PKCS#1 v1.5 encryption in new designs because of its history of oracle issues. For key exchange in TLS 1.3, RSA key transport was removed entirely in favor of (EC)DHE.

| Security target (approx.) | RSA modulus | ECC (see §5) |
|:---|:---:|:---:|
| ~128-bit | 3072-bit or higher | ~256-bit |
| ~192-bit | 7680-bit or higher | ~384-bit |
| ~256-bit | 15360-bit or higher | ~521-bit |

## 4. Diffie-Hellman Key Exchange

DH lets Alice and Bob agree on a shared secret over a public channel without ever sending the secret itself (Wong, Ch.5).

### Public parameters
- A safe prime $p$.
- A generator $g$ of a large subgroup of order $q$ (to avoid small-subgroup attacks).

### Protocol
$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Alice: } & a \stackrel{\$}{\leftarrow} \mathbb{Z}_q,\quad A \leftarrow g^a \bmod p \\
\text{Bob: } & b \stackrel{\$}{\leftarrow} \mathbb{Z}_q,\quad B \leftarrow g^b \bmod p \\
\text{Shared: } & K_A \leftarrow B^a = g^{ba},\quad K_B \leftarrow A^b = g^{ab}
\end{aligned}
$$

Because exponents commute: $K_A = K_B = g^{ab} \bmod p$.

### Hardness
An observer sees $(p,g,A,B)$, but computing $g^{ab}$ is the Computational Diffie-Hellman (CDH) problem, closely related to the DLP.

```
Alice                                              Bob
  │ a random                                         │ b random
  │ A = g^a mod p                                    │ B = g^b mod p
  │─────────────── A ───────────────────────────────>│
  │<────────────── B ────────────────────────────────│
  │ K = B^a                                          │ K = A^b
  │═══════════════ K = g^{ab} ═══════════════════════│
```

PFS (Perfect Forward Secrecy): use an ephemeral key (DHE/ECDHE) per session, so a future leak of a long-term key does not open past sessions.

Always validate that public keys lie in the correct subgroup. Use standardized groups (for example RFC 7919 FFDHE) or, better, elliptic curves.

## 5. Elliptic Curve Cryptography (ECC)

An elliptic curve over a prime field $p$ (short Weierstrass form):

$$
y^2 \equiv x^3 + ax + b \pmod p
$$

with non-singular discriminant $4a^3+27b^2 \not\equiv 0 \pmod p$. The points on the curve, plus the point at infinity $\mathcal{O}$, form an abelian group.

### Group law (geometric intuition, then the formula)
For $P=(x_1,y_1)$, $Q=(x_2,y_2)$, $P+Q=(x_3,y_3)$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda &=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1} & P\neq Q \\[6pt]
\dfrac{3x_1^2+a}{2y_1} & P=Q \text{ (doubling)}
\end{cases}
\pmod p \\[4pt]
x_3 &= \lambda^2 - x_1 - x_2,\qquad
y_3 &= \lambda(x_1-x_3)-y_1
\end{aligned}
$$

Scalar multiplication: $k\cdot G = \underbrace{G+\cdots+G}_{k}$ (double-and-add). Easy in one direction; the inverse (ECDLP) is hard.

### ECDH
$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Alice: } & A = a\cdot G \\
\text{Bob: } & B = b\cdot G \\
\text{Shared: } & a\cdot B = b\cdot A = (ab)\cdot G
\end{aligned}
$$

Common curves: P-256 / prime256v1 / secp256r1. A modern alternative is X25519 (Montgomery curve, RFC 7748), whose API is harder to misuse.

ECC matches RSA security with much smaller keys and faster operations, which makes it ideal for TLS, SSH, and mobile. Prefer high-level APIs (`X25519`, `Ed25519`) over raw point arithmetic.

| Level | Symmetric | RSA | ECC |
|:---|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| Standard | 128 | 3072 | 256 (P-256 / X25519) |
| High | 192 | 7680 | 384 |
| Ultra | 256 | 15360 | 521 / Ed448 |

## 6. Digital Signatures

### Model
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sigma &\leftarrow \mathsf{Sign}_{sk}(m) \\
\{0,1\} &\leftarrow \mathsf{Verify}_{pk}(m,\sigma)
\end{aligned}
$$

### RSA-PSS (the recommended RSA signature scheme)
Probabilistic Signature Scheme, with stronger security proofs than PKCS#1 v1.5.

### ECDSA (brief)
With order $n$ of base point $G$, private key $d$, and $Q=dG$:

1. Choose a nonce $k \stackrel{\$}{\leftarrow} \mathbb{Z}_n^*$, which must be unique and secret. Reusing $k$ leaks the private key, as in the classic PlayStation 3 case.
2. Compute $r = (kG)_x \bmod n$.
3. Compute $s = k^{-1}(H(m)+d\cdot r) \bmod n$.
4. The signature is $(r,s)$.

Verification uses $u_1=H(m)s^{-1}$, $u_2=rs^{-1}$, then checks $(u_1G+u_2Q)_x \equiv r$.

### EdDSA (Ed25519)
A deterministic, hash-derived nonce removes the class of RNG bugs that affect ECDSA. It is the modern standard for SSH, Git, and many other protocols.

## 7. Hybrid Encryption (KEM + DEM)

Encrypting a large file with plain RSA is inefficient and not idiomatic. The standard pattern is:

1. Generate a session key $k \stackrel{\$}{\leftarrow} \{0,1\}^{256}$.
2. Encrypt the data: $c_{\text{data}} \leftarrow \mathsf{AEAD}_k(m)$ (AES-GCM / ChaCha20-Poly1305).
3. Wrap $k$: $c_k \leftarrow \mathsf{Enc}_{pk}(k)$ (RSA-OAEP), or derive $k$ from an ECDH shared secret via HKDF.

This is the KEM/DEM pattern (Key Encapsulation + Data Encapsulation), which also underlies ECIES (Wong §6.4).

TLS, Signal, and most modern messaging apps follow hybrid designs: asymmetric for agreement and authentication, symmetric AEAD for records.

---

## 8. Quantum Threats and PQC (Outlook)

Shor's algorithm breaks factoring and DLP/ECDLP on a large-scale quantum computer, putting RSA, DH, and ECC at long-term risk.

The industry direction (NIST PQC): ML-KEM (Kyber) for key encapsulation, ML-DSA (Dilithium) and SLH-DSA for signatures. Hybrid deployment (classical combined with PQC) is already being trialed in experimental TLS.

Symmetric ciphers (AES-256) and hashes (SHA-256) are comparatively more resistant. Grover's algorithm only gives a quadratic speedup, so AES-256 retains a healthy security margin.

## 9. OpenSSL Cheatsheet

```bash
# ── RSA 4096 keypair ─────────────────────────────────────────
openssl genrsa -out private_rsa.pem 4096
openssl rsa -in private_rsa.pem -pubout -out public_rsa.pem

# ── Encrypt/decrypt a small blob with OAEP (not textbook RSA) ─
openssl pkeyutl -encrypt -pubin -inkey public_rsa.pem \
  -pkeyopt rsa_padding_mode:oaep -in key.bin -out key.enc
openssl pkeyutl -decrypt -inkey private_rsa.pem \
  -pkeyopt rsa_padding_mode:oaep -in key.enc -out key.bin

# ── ECC P-256 / Ed25519 ──────────────────────────────────────
openssl ecparam -name prime256v1 -genkey -noout -out private_ec.pem
openssl genpkey -algorithm ED25519 -out private_ed25519.pem
openssl pkey -in private_ed25519.pem -pubout -out public_ed25519.pem

# ── Sign & verify (RSA-SHA256) ───────────────────────────────
openssl dgst -sha256 -sign private_rsa.pem -out doc.sig document.pdf
openssl dgst -sha256 -verify public_rsa.pem -signature doc.sig document.pdf
```

## Security Notes

| Risk | Cause | Mitigation |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Textbook RSA | No padding | OAEP; do not use raw RSA |
| ECDSA nonce reuse | Weak RNG | Ed25519 or RFC 6979 |
| Small subgroup | Weak point/group validation | Standard curves plus checks |
| Static RSA in TLS | No PFS | ECDHE only (TLS 1.3) |
| Key theft | Disk/backup leak | HSM, passphrase, short-lived keys |

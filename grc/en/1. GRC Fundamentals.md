## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|:---|:---|
| Governance | How an organization sets the direction, responsibilities, and rules for security |
| Risk | The likelihood of something bad happening, multiplied by its impact |
| Compliance | Proving that the organization meets laws, standards, and contracts |
| Control | A measure that reduces risk, such as MFA, backups, or training |
| Risk register | A list of risks with their owners, ratings, and treatment plans |
| Risk appetite | How much risk management is willing to accept |
| ISMS | Information Security Management System: the security management system defined by ISO 27001 |
| Gap analysis | Comparing the current state with what a standard requires |
| Evidence | Documents, logs, or screenshots showing that a control actually operates |
| RTO / RPO | How long a service may be down / how much data may be lost after a disaster |

## 1. What GRC is

Most Zero2Root lessons are about tools and commands. GRC answers a different question: **what security is needed, who is responsible for it, and how do we prove it**.

| Part | Question | Example output |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Governance | Who decides what? What are the rules? | Security policy, committee structure, the CISO role |
| Risk | What is most likely to hurt us, and how badly? | Risk register, annual risk assessment |
| Compliance | Do we meet the laws and standards, and can we prove it? | Audit reports, an ISO 27001 certificate, a SOC 2 report |

GRC work deals with people and documents more than with a terminal. But a GRC analyst who understands the technical side is far more useful, because they can tell a control that really works from one that exists only on paper.

## 2. Policies, standards, procedures, guidelines

Security documents have levels. Mixing them up is a common mistake.

| Document | Nature | Example |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Policy | Mandatory, short, states "what" and "why", approved by top management | "All access to company systems must use multi-factor authentication." |
| Standard | Mandatory, more specific | "MFA uses an authenticator app or a FIDO2 key. SMS is not allowed for admins." |
| Procedure | Mandatory, step by step | "How to enroll MFA for a new employee: 1. ... 2. ..." |
| Guideline | Advice, not mandatory | "Using a password manager is recommended." |

Policies rarely change. Standards and procedures can be updated more often without asking the board for approval each time.

Policies most organizations have: acceptable use, access control, data classification, password management, incident response ([LC-075](../../blue-team/en/4.%20Incident%20Response%20%26%20Digital%20Forensics.md)), backup, and vendor management.

## 3. Risk management

### The parts of a risk

```
 Asset  ×  Threat   ×  Vulnerability   →   Risk
 (what we    (who or what     (a weakness
  protect)    attacks)         that can be used)
```

Example: asset = customer database, threat = a ransomware group, vulnerability = an unpatched server with RDP exposed.

### Rating a risk

The most common method is a **likelihood × impact** matrix, each on a scale of 1 to 5:

| Likelihood \ Impact | 1 Minor | 2 | 3 Moderate | 4 | 5 Severe |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| 5 Almost certain | 5 | 10 | 15 | 20 | 25 |
| 4 | 4 | 8 | 12 | 16 | 20 |
| 3 Possible | 3 | 6 | 9 | 12 | 15 |
| 2 | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 |
| 1 Rare | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |

For example, 15 and above is high, 8 to 14 is medium, below 8 is low. The thresholds come from the organization's risk appetite.

The matrix is simple and easy to communicate, but subjective. More mature organizations use quantitative methods such as FAIR, which express risk in money per year. Hubbard & Seiersen's book covers this.

### Four ways to treat a risk

| Option | Meaning | Example |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Mitigate | Add controls that lower the likelihood or the impact | Patch the server, close RDP, enable MFA |
| Transfer | Shift part of the impact to someone else | Cyber insurance, vendor contracts |
| Accept | Knowingly decide not to act, with written approval | A legacy system to be retired in 3 months |
| Avoid | Stop the activity that creates the risk | No longer storing card numbers |

The risk that remains after controls are in place is the **residual risk**. Residual risk must be approved by the risk owner, not quietly by the IT team.

### Risk register

| ID | Risk | Owner | L | I | Score | Treatment | Status |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| R-01 | Ransomware gets in through RDP exposed on the file server | Head of IT | 4 | 5 | 20 | Mitigate: close RDP, VPN + MFA, offline backups | In progress |
| R-02 | Customer data leaks from a public cloud bucket | Head of Engineering | 3 | 5 | 15 | Mitigate: Block Public Access, monthly Prowler audit ([LC-077](../../cloud-security/en/1.%20Cloud%20Security%20Fundamentals.md)) | Done |
| R-03 | An employee falls for a payment-fraud phishing email | Finance Director | 3 | 4 | 12 | Mitigate: phone verification for large transfers, training ([LC-068](../../social-engineering/en/Social%20Engineering%20%E2%80%94%20Principles%2C%20Vectors%20%26%20Defense.md)) | In progress |

Every risk needs an **owner** who can make decisions, not just "the IT team".

## 4. Security controls

Controls are grouped in two ways.

**By function:**

| Type | Purpose | Example |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Preventive | Stops an event | MFA, firewall, training |
| Detective | Notices an event | SIEM, alerts on odd logins ([LC-072](../../blue-team/en/1.%20SOC%20%26%20Log%20Analysis.md)), audit logs |
| Corrective | Recovers after an event | Restoring backups, the incident response plan |
| Deterrent | Makes an attacker think twice | Login warning banners, written sanctions |
| Compensating | A substitute when the main control cannot be used | Network isolation for an old machine that cannot be patched |

**By form:**

| Form | Example |
|:---|:---|
| Administrative | Policies, procedures, training, background checks |
| Technical | Encryption, MFA, EDR, firewalls |
| Physical | Server room locks, CCTV, access badges |

Good defense uses several layers of different kinds, so if one fails another still works (defense in depth).

## 5. Frameworks you will meet

| Framework | From | Purpose | Cost of the document |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| NIST CSF 2.0 | NIST (US) | A general framework for organizing a security program | Free |
| ISO/IEC 27001:2022 | ISO | A certifiable ISMS standard | Paid |
| CIS Controls v8 | Center for Internet Security | 18 technical controls ordered by priority | Free |
| SOC 2 | AICPA (US) | An audit report for service providers, often requested by SaaS customers | Paid |
| PCI DSS v4 | PCI SSC | Mandatory for anyone processing payment cards | Free |

### NIST CSF 2.0

Six core functions:

| Function | Question | Example activities |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Govern (GV) | Who is responsible, and what is the strategy? | Policies, roles, supply-chain risk management |
| Identify (ID) | What do we have, and what are the risks? | Asset inventory, risk assessment |
| Protect (PR) | How do we prevent incidents? | Access control, training, data security |
| Detect (DE) | How do we know something happened? | Monitoring, anomaly analysis |
| Respond (RS) | What do we do when it happens? | Incident response, communication |
| Recover (RC) | How do we get back to normal? | Restoring, improving processes |

Govern was added in version 2.0 (2024) to make it clear that security is a leadership responsibility, not only a technical one.

### ISO/IEC 27001:2022

ISO 27001 asks an organization to build an ISMS: a repeating process of assessing risk, choosing controls, measuring, and improving. Annex A contains 93 controls in 4 themes: organizational, people, physical, and technological. The organization chooses which controls apply and records the reasons in a **Statement of Applicability (SoA)**.

ISO 27001 certification is granted by a certification body after an audit. It is valid for three years, with a surveillance audit every year.

## 6. Data protection law

An organization that processes personal data has legal duties, including notification if a breach happens.

| | Indonesia | Japan | European Union |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| Law | Law No. 27 of 2022 on Personal Data Protection (UU PDP) | Act on the Protection of Personal Information (APPI) | General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) |
| Fully in force | October 2024 | Latest amendment in force April 2022 | May 2018 |
| Regulator | The personal data protection agency | Personal Information Protection Commission (PPC) | A data protection authority in each country |
| Breach notification deadline | Within 3 × 24 hours to data subjects and the agency | A prompt preliminary report (generally 3 to 5 days) and a full report within 30 days (60 days for deliberate attacks) to the PPC, plus notice to affected people | 72 hours to the authority |

What all three share: collect only what you need, use it for its stated purpose, protect it with reasonable controls, and have a rehearsed breach notification procedure. Short deadlines mean the procedure has to be ready **before** an incident, not written during one.

Note: this summary is for learning. For real decisions, check the latest legal text and consult your legal team, because the implementing regulations are still evolving.

## 7. Audits and evidence

To an auditor, a control that cannot be proven does not exist.

| Control | Weak evidence | Strong evidence |
|:---|:---|:---|
| All admins use MFA | "Yes, everyone uses MFA." | An export of admins with MFA status from the identity system, with the date it was taken |
| Backups are tested | The backup policy | Monthly restore test reports with time, system, and result |
| Access is revoked when staff leave | The offboarding procedure | A sample of 10 leavers, matching leaving dates against account-disabled dates |
| Critical patches applied within 14 days | The patch schedule | Vulnerability scanner reports from two consecutive periods |

A typical audit flow:

1. **Scope**: which systems, locations, and period are audited.
2. **Gap analysis**: compare the current state with the standard.
3. **Design test**: on paper, is the control enough to reduce the risk?
4. **Operating effectiveness test**: did the control actually work throughout the period? Tested with samples.
5. **Findings and remediation plan**: every finding has an owner and a deadline.

Internal audits are done by the organization's own team as preparation. External audits are done by an independent party, for example for ISO 27001 certification or a SOC 2 report.

Collecting evidence automatically, such as regular exports of Prowler results or MFA status, is far lighter than gathering screenshots right before an audit.

## 8. Third-party risk

Many large breaches start at a vendor: an IT service provider, a SaaS application, or a contractor with access to systems.

| Stage | Activities |
|:---|:---|
| Before the contract | Security questionnaire, ask for a SOC 2 report or ISO 27001 certificate, classify the vendor by the data and access it gets |
| Contract | Security clauses: incident notification duties, right to audit, data location, data deletion when the contract ends |
| During the contract | Periodic reviews according to the vendor's risk level, minimal access |
| End of the contract | Revoke all access, ask for proof of data deletion |

A vendor that holds your customers' data is still your responsibility in the eyes of the law and your customers.

## 9. Business continuity

| Term | Meaning |
|:---|:---|
| BIA (Business Impact Analysis) | Identifying critical business processes and the impact if they stop |
| RTO (Recovery Time Objective) | How long a service may be down before the impact becomes unacceptable |
| RPO (Recovery Point Objective) | How much data, measured in time, may be lost |
| BCP | Business Continuity Plan: how the business keeps running during a disruption |
| DRP | Disaster Recovery Plan: how IT systems are restored |

Example: a point-of-sale system has an RTO of 4 hours and an RPO of 15 minutes. Backups must run at least every 15 minutes, and the restore process must have been tested to finish in under 4 hours.

A plan that has never been tested is only a document. A tabletop exercise, a simulated incident talked through with management around a table, is a cheap way to find its gaps.

## 10. Practice

### Task 1: concepts

1. What is the difference between a policy and a procedure?
2. Name the four ways to treat a risk.
3. Which function was added in NIST CSF 2.0?
4. Under UU PDP, what is the deadline for notifying a personal data breach?

<details><summary>Answer key</summary>

1. A policy states what is mandatory and why, is approved by top management, and rarely changes. A procedure gives the step-by-step way to do it.
2. Mitigate, transfer, accept, avoid.
3. Govern.
4. Within 3 × 24 hours.

</details>

### Task 2: build a risk register

A small online shop:

- The website runs on one VPS, and the admin logs in to the panel with a password only.
- Database backups are stored on the same VPS.
- Payments go through a payment gateway; the shop stores no card numbers.
- Customer data: names, addresses, phone numbers.
- A freelance developer has full SSH access and stopped working with the shop 6 months ago.

Write at least three risks with likelihood and impact scores (1 to 5), an owner, and a treatment.

<details><summary>Example answer</summary>

| Risk | L | I | Score | Treatment |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| Admin account taken over because there is no MFA | 4 | 4 | 16 | Mitigate: MFA for admins, restrict the admin panel to specific IPs |
| Total data loss because backups sit on the same server (ransomware or disk failure) | 3 | 5 | 15 | Mitigate: daily backups to another location, monthly restore tests |
| The old freelancer's access is misused | 3 | 5 | 15 | Mitigate: remove the SSH key today, write an offboarding procedure |
| A customer data leak triggers UU PDP duties | 2 | 4 | 8 | Mitigate: a 3 × 24 hour notification procedure, data encryption |
| Payment card risk | 1 | 4 | 4 | Transfer: already handled by the payment gateway |

In a small shop the owner of every risk is usually the shop owner.

</details>

### Task 3: a small gap analysis

Map the controls the shop in Task 2 already has to the six NIST CSF 2.0 functions. Which function is weakest?

<details><summary>Example answer</summary>

| Function | What exists | What is missing |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Govern | Nothing | A short policy and a person responsible for security |
| Identify | Partial knowledge of assets | A list of assets and who has access |
| Protect | Admin password | MFA, offboarding, encryption |
| Detect | Nothing | Admin login alerts, uptime monitoring |
| Respond | Nothing | An incident and UU PDP notification procedure |
| Recover | Backups on the same server | Separate, tested backups |

The weakest are Govern, Detect, and Respond. The shop would not know it had been breached and would not know what to do next.

</details>

  Governance: who decides · Risk: what hurts most · Compliance: can be proven

  Policy (mandatory, what & why) > Standard (mandatory, specific) > Procedure (steps) > Guideline (advice)

  Asset × Threat × Vulnerability · Score = Likelihood × Impact (1-5 × 1-5)

  Preventive · Detective · Corrective · Deterrent · Compensating
